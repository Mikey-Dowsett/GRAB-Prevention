# Westbrook University — Anti-Harassment, Discrimination, and Title IX Policy
**Policy Number:** HR-POL-010  
**Effective Date:** August 1, 2022  
**Last Revised:** January 10, 2024  
**Applies to:** All university employees, students, contractors, and visitors

---

## 1. Statement of Policy

Westbrook University is committed to maintaining an environment free from harassment, discrimination, and retaliation. The university prohibits unlawful harassment and discrimination based on race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability, veteran status, pregnancy, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state, or local law.

Harassment and discrimination by or against any member of the university community — including faculty, staff, students, contractors, and guests — will not be tolerated. This policy applies to conduct that occurs on university property, at university-sponsored events, and in any context connected to the employment or educational relationship.

---

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Harassment
Unwelcome verbal, physical, or visual conduct that:
- Is based on a protected characteristic, AND
- Is severe or pervasive enough to create a hostile, intimidating, or offensive work or educational environment, OR
- Results in an adverse employment or academic decision

A single incident may constitute harassment if it is sufficiently severe (e.g., physical assault, explicit sexual images sent to a coworker).

### 2.2 Sexual Harassment
Sexual harassment includes, but is not limited to:
- Unwanted sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature when:
  - Submission is made a condition of employment or academic standing (quid pro quo), OR
  - The conduct creates a hostile environment

Sexual harassment may be perpetrated by individuals of any gender against individuals of any gender.

### 2.3 Title IX
Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972 prohibits sex-based discrimination in any educational program or activity receiving federal financial assistance. Westbrook University receives federal funding and is therefore subject to Title IX. The Title IX Coordinator oversees the university's compliance with Title IX obligations.

### 2.4 Retaliation
Retaliation against any individual who in good faith reports harassment, participates in an investigation, or exercises any right under this policy is strictly prohibited and may itself constitute grounds for disciplinary action.

## 3. Reporting

### 3.1 Who to Report To

Any employee who believes they have experienced or witnessed harassment, discrimination, or retaliation may report to any of the following:
- Their direct supervisor (if not the alleged harasser)
- The Office of Human Resources: hr@westbrook.edu / (816) 555-0101
- The Office of Equity and Inclusion: equity@westbrook.edu / (816) 555-0190
- University Ethics Hotline (anonymous): 1-800-555-0182

Reports involving sexual misconduct, dating violence, domestic violence, or stalking are governed by the Title IX process and should be directed to the Title IX Coordinator.

### 3.2 Responsible Employees

Most university employees — including all supervisors, faculty, and administrators — are "Responsible Employees" who are required to report any information they receive about potential Title IX violations to the Title IX Coordinator. Responsible Employees cannot guarantee confidentiality to individuals who disclose Title IX concerns.

## 4. Investigation Process

### 4.1 Initial Review
Upon receiving a report, HR or the Office of Equity and Inclusion will conduct an initial intake review within 5 business days to determine whether the reported conduct falls within the scope of this policy.

### 4.2 Formal Investigation
If a formal investigation is warranted, an investigator will be assigned. The investigator will:
- Notify the respondent (alleged harasser) of the allegations
- Interview the complainant, respondent, and relevant witnesses
- Review available documents and evidence
- Prepare a written investigative report with findings

Both the complainant and respondent have the right to:
- Be accompanied by an advisor (including an attorney) during any meeting
- Review the draft investigation report and submit a written response before it is finalized
- Appeal the final determination (see Section 4.4)

### 4.3 Timeframe
The university aims to complete investigations within 60 calendar days of the initial report. Extensions may be granted for complex cases or due to scheduling constraints.

### 4.4 Appeal
Either party may appeal the final determination within 10 business days of receiving the written outcome. Appeals must be based on one of the following grounds:
- Procedural error that materially affected the outcome
- New evidence not reasonably available during the investigation
- The sanction imposed is grossly disproportionate to the findings

Appeals are reviewed by the Vice Provost for Academic Affairs (for faculty) or the Vice President for Human Resources (for staff).

---

## 5. Interim Measures

While an investigation is pending, the university may implement interim measures to protect the safety and well-being of the parties. These may include:
- Temporary schedule or location changes
- No-contact directives
- Administrative leave (with pay) for the respondent
- Referral to the EAP

Interim measures are not disciplinary and do not constitute a finding of responsibility.

---

## 6. Sanctions

If the investigation concludes that a policy violation occurred, the university will impose appropriate sanctions. For employees, sanctions may include:
- Mandatory training
- Written reprimand
- Suspension without pay
- Demotion
- Termination of employment

The severity of the sanction will be commensurate with the nature and severity of the violation, the respondent's prior conduct history, and any mitigating or aggravating factors.

---

## 7. Training Requirements

All employees must complete the Title IX and Anti-Harassment training (HR-103) within 30 days of hire and every two years thereafter. Supervisors must additionally complete the Supervisor-Specific Harassment Prevention module (HR-113) upon promotion and every two years. Training completion is tracked in Cornerstone.
